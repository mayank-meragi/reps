# Health algorithm decision log

Every health computation is recorded here: the options considered, the choice, sources, constants, validation and open issues. The process is in `AGENTS.md` ("Health algorithms"). A shipped algorithm is only changed by repeating that process.

Status key: **shipped** (in the app), **needs review** (shipped before the research-and-approval rule existed, to be revisited with options and sources before anyone builds on it).

## Sleep score — shipped, approved 11 Oct 2026

- Code: `src/lib/health/sleepScore.ts`. Command: `health.get_scores` (`sleep`).
- Choice: option C, a Whoop-style "hours vs the night's need" score. Duration 55 (minutes asleep vs need tonight, where need tonight is the user's sleep need plus 25% of last night's shortfall; no strain adder), regularity 15 (mean of bedtime and wake-time distance from the average of up to the previous 7 nights; full within 15 min, zero at 90; needs 4 nights), restlessness 10 (brief wake-ups per hour in bed; full at 0.5 or fewer, zero at 3; stage-tracked nights only), efficiency 5 (full at 92% or more, zero at 75%), restorative 15 (deep 13% and REM 20% floors, 7.5 each, as before). Missing parts are dropped and the rest rescaled. Naps are not counted.
- Options compared on the user's data (6 nights, 5–10 Oct 2026, scores 5th to 10th): A previous score (94, 90, 85, 98, 90, 85); B Oura-style bounded contributors (91, 92, 85, 93, 89, 86); C Whoop-style with strain adder (88, 77, 75, 91, 86, 64); D Fitbit-style duration/quality/restoration (87, 88, 78, 92, 85, 78); E duration plus regularity only (87, 80, 73, 96, 83, 58). Shipped C without the strain adder and with the weights above scores 89, 85, 77, 90, 84, 71.
- Why: on this device efficiency (96–99%) and stage shares sit at full marks almost every night, so the old score moved with duration alone and gave a 5 h night 85. Duration is the best-supported input (AASM/SRS 7 h or more; Oura calls it the most significant); regularity predicts mortality about as well as duration (Windred 2024); stage accuracy is only moderate against polysomnography (kappa about 0.4–0.5), so stages carry little weight.
- Sources: [AASM/SRS consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25979105/); [NSF sleep quality indicators, Ohayon 2017](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1172405); [Windred 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10782501/); [wearable stage accuracy review 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11004611/); [Oura](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025445574); [Whoop](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/how-does-this-new-algorithm-improve-my-sleep-tracking-compared-to-the-old-version/490); [Fitbit](https://support.google.com/fitbit/answer/14236513); [Garmin](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/blog/?p=34517). Vendor weights are unpublished, so weights here are chosen, not copied or fitted.
- Validation: none against a reference yet; 6 nights. Fitbit Air reports 0 minutes to fall asleep, so latency is not used.
- Open: validate against Bevel's daily sleep score once supplied; decide whether naps count toward the night; whether a strain adder to need is worth adding; Recovery takes this score at 20%, so its numbers shift with this change (its baseline recomputes from the new scores).

## Recovery score — shipped, approved 11 Oct 2026

- Code: `src/lib/health/recoveryScore.ts`. Command: `health.get_scores` (`recovery`).
- Choice: option A, a weighted z-composite. Last night's HRV (log RMSSD), resting heart rate, sleep score and respiratory rate are each compared, as a z-score clamped to ±3, with the user's own previous 60 days. Weights HRV 40, resting HR 25, sleep 20, respiratory 10, rescaled over the inputs present. An S-curve rescaled so z −3 gives 0 and z +3 gives 100 (z 0 is 50), clamped to 1–100, minus up to 5 points each for unusual skin temperature or low SpO2. Zones: red under 34, yellow 34–66, green 67 and up.
- The three changes made on approval (from the first version):
  1. Baseline 60 days (was 30). No estimate under 5 days; a provisional estimate (`provisionalScore`, not headlined) from 5 to 13; the score from 14.
  2. Night gate: a night under 4 hours' sleep gets no score and is left out of every baseline.
  3. Top end: the curve is rescaled so very high HRV can reach 100 (it topped out near 97).
- Options compared on the user's data (7 nights, 4–10 Oct 2026; Bevel showed 100 on 9 Oct, other days unknown): A weighted z-composite, B HRV-only normal range (HRV4Training, Garmin, Plews), C bounded contributors (Oura, Polar, Fitbit), D A plus yesterday's strain (Garmin Training Readiness, Oura). On 9 Oct: A 87, B 97, C 95, D 93. C compressed every day into 80–95; B swung most. D rejected because HRV and resting HR already carry yesterday's load and Google removed activity from its readiness score for that reason.
- Sources: Bevel help ([recovery](https://help.bevel.health/en/articles/11258177), [glossary](https://help.bevel.health/en/articles/11251073)); [Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/podcast-84-recovery-update/); [Oura](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057791533); [Garmin HRV status](https://www.garmin.com/en-CA/blog/?p=31344); [Polar](https://support.polar.com/en/nightly-recharge-recovery-measurement); [Google readiness](https://support.google.com/googlehealth/answer/14236710); [Athlytic](https://athlyticapp.helpscoutdocs.com/article/20-understanding-recovery); [HRV4Training](https://www.hrv4training.com/blog2/determining-your-normal-range); [Buchheit 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3936188/); [Plews et al.](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/d7b41d29-e92e-4acf-90ea-ed68d84d6c73).
- Constants (weights 40/25/20/10, curve steepness 1.2, SD floors, 4 h gate, 14-day gate, 5-point flag penalty) are chosen from the research above, not fitted to a reference. Bevel's published method gives no weights or cut-offs.
- Validation: none against a reference yet. Only 7 nights exist, the first partial, and the user's night-to-night lnHRV varies about 15% against 4–8% in the literature.
- Open: fit or validate against Bevel's daily Recovery values once the user supplies them; revisit the weights (Whoop says HRV dominates and that sleep and RHR are largely redundant with it); the Health Monitor's Lower/Higher badges still use a 30-day baseline, unchanged and not yet reviewed; the Strain target band uses recovery's early estimate (marked provisional) until recovery shows a score at 14 baseline days.

## Strain — shipped, reviewed and kept 11 Oct 2026

- Code: `src/lib/health/strainScore.ts`. Command: `health.get_scores` (`strain`).
- Choice (unchanged): `load = Σ minutes × max(0, bpm − 110)`, `strain = 100 × (1 − e^(−load/1234))`, from the all-day heart-rate histogram. 110 bpm is 40% of heart rate reserve for a resting rate of 60 and max of 185 (the user's implied values from their Fitbit zones). Target band set by the day's recovery zone (red under 35%, yellow 35–60%, green 60–85%); from recovery's early estimate, marked provisional, while recovery has 5–13 baseline days (decided 11 Oct 2026).
- Options compared on the user's data (7 days, 3–9 Oct 2026, against Bevel's strain 34, 17, 89, 12, 13, 10, 16; scale fitted per option; average miss fit / held-out): A linear excess over 110 bpm 2.4 / 3.2 (chosen); B Banister TRIMP as in Google Cardio Load (HRR, 30% floor, k 1.92) 8.3 / 8.5; C Edwards HRR zones weighted 1–5 3.6 / 4.7; D Whoop-style log of Banister 8.7 / 9.5; E Fitbit zone minutes 1/2/3 6.4 / 8.7. B and D flatten the one hard day (52–53 vs Bevel's 89). Garmin/Firstbeat EPOC and Whoop are proprietary and not reproducible.
- Threshold decision: the daily Fitbit MODERATE zone floor (113–116 bpm since 3 Oct; 110 before the device) was tested in place of a fixed 110 and fit worse (3.9 / 7.2 with the scale refit to 986; 6.6 at the current scale), so the fixed 110 was kept. The literature gives no single threshold for all-day load; the 40% heart rate reserve convention matches ACSM's moderate floor (Garber 2011), Fitbit's moderate zone and the end of Google's 30–40% down-weight band.
- Sources: [Google Cardio Load, Phillips et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11613v2); [Fitbit zone method](https://www.wareable.com/fitbit/active-zone-minutes-explained-7968); [ACSM intensity classes (tables)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8581515/table/T1); [TRIMP method comparison, Nakamura 2010](https://www.periodicos.rc.biblioteca.unesp.br/index.php/motriz/article/view/1980-6574.2010v16n2p311); [TRIMP reliability](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/41982); [modified TRIMP for intermittent sport](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/training-load-quantification-in-elite-swimmers-using-a-modified-v/); [free-living heart rate review, Schumann 2025](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/100165); [Whoop strain overview](https://www.whoop.com/thelocker/how-does-whoop-strain-work-101/); [Whoop accuracy review](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.01.04.24300784v1.full); [Polar Training Load Pro](https://support.polar.com/us-en/training-load-pro).
- Validation: 2.4 average miss on the data it was fitted to and 3.2 held out, from 7 days dominated by one workout day. The earlier log said about 8 held out, from a different fitting loss; both are small-sample.
- Constants (110 bpm, 1234) come from fitting to Bevel's numbers, not from a physiological model. The fit on the 7 days gives a scale near 1090; 1234 was kept rather than refit on so few days.
- Open: re-fit as more Bevel days arrive; personalise the threshold (40% of reserve) once a max heart rate or age is available; the target bands (0–35 / 35–60 / 60–85) are an unvalidated guess, and need Bevel's daily target range alongside its recovery to test; the histogram cannot filter minutes without movement as Google's method does.
